The offer, and its boundary

A business address, mail and document handling by on-site staff, and five bookable meeting rooms. Stated plainly across the site as the whole service.

What is deliberately not offered. Company registration or incorporation, SEC or BIR filings, business permits, bookkeeping, accounting, tax preparation, payroll, corporate secretarial work, or business-setup advice. There are no partner firms delivering any of it.

This appears on the About page, the Terms, and a site-wide footer notice, and is enforced in the chatbot’s guardrails. It is a positioning choice, not an omission.

Requirements, and the rule behind them

Registered-address use requires Philippine registration — SEC for a corporation or partnership, DTI for a sole proprietor. Correspondence use does not: identity and KYC only. So anyone without Philippine registration starts on the Address package and moves up once it exists.

ApplicantSubmitsPackageRegistered now?
Philippine corporation or partnershipSEC registration, KYC form, two valid IDs, paymentAnyYes

Foreign company, already PH-registeredPhilippine SEC registration, KYC form, two valid IDs, paymentAnyYes
Foreign company or individual, pre-entryHome-country registration, KYC form, paymentAddressNo — upgrade later

Stated prominently for pre-entry clients. A correspondence address is not a Philippine registered office. It does not register a company here and does not authorise doing business in the Philippines — that requires registration with the SEC and the relevant agencies, arranged with the client’s own advisers.

This wording should go to counsel alongside the service agreement, together with the current SEC and foreign-ownership rules on when a foreign company is considered to be doing business in the Philippines.

Two structural gaps

No social proof of any kind. No testimonials, client logos, review count or case examples. For a product whose value is credibility, this is the largest commercial gap — but the service is new, so it should be earned rather than manufactured. A Google Business Profile and a request to the first ten clients at the sixty-day mark is the honest route.

No client portal. Mail notifications, room bookings and invoices are handled by email. A deliberate choice rather than an oversight, but worth revisiting as the client base grows.

Two settings intentionally off. Search indexing is blocked, so the site is a private staging build rather than a public launch. The chatbot is built and switched off, waiting on FAQ sign-off — its knowledge base builds itself from the content files, so it will reflect whatever the site says on the day it is enabled.
